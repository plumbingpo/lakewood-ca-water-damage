Kitchen Water Damage Help in Lakewood

No room has as many water connections as the kitchen, with its faucet, drain, disposal, dishwasher and refrigerator line. Almost every one sits out of view, behind an appliance or inside a cabinet. When one drips, the water runs to the back of the cabinet base and spreads under the floor. For homeowners in Lakewood, our job is to track it down, dry it out, and preserve all of the kitchen that can be saved.

Cabinets are the expensive part of this problem. Particleboard boxes swell fast and stay swollen, but solid wood and plywood frequently dry well. By removing toe kicks and drilling small holes where they will not show, we push dry air beneath and behind the cabinets, and in many kitchens the countertops and cabinets never move.

Signs to Look For

  • Doors on the cabinets that have gone out of alignment or started sticking
  • Toe kicks that have swollen, darkened, or pulled away from the cabinet base
  • Laminate or wood floor that is lifting or cupping where you stand at the dishwasher
  • A sink cabinet bottom that feels soft, looks stained, or has warped
  • Behind the dishwasher or sink, a wall with staining or blistered paint
  • The ceiling under the kitchen has developed a brown ring

What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

Behind the Dishwasher

A slow drip at the supply or drain connection wets the subfloor and the cabinet sides next to it. Until the floor changes, the dishwasher covers everything.

Under the Base Cabinets

The gap under base cabinets is closed on every side. Water collects there, stays for weeks, and grows mold that nobody sees until the cabinets are pulled.

The Wall Behind the Cabinets

Supply and drain lines run through the wall behind the sink and dishwasher. Cabinets fully cover that drywall and framing, so a leak can soak them and mold can spread out of sight.

Layers of Flooring

It is common for a kitchen floor to be laid right over the previous one. Trapped water sits between the two, with no way to evaporate and no sign on the surface.

If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

First hour

Water Runs Under

Water from a failed hose or fitting flows to the back of the cabinets and under the appliances. It slips beneath flooring through the seams and edges.

Hours 24 to 48

Cabinets Begin to Swell

The water is absorbed by particleboard bases and toe kicks, which swell. Behind the dishwasher and beneath the cabinets, the closed, dark space allows mold to begin.

Day 3 to 7

Floors Begin Moving

Laminate swells at the edges, hardwood cups, and vinyl lifts once the adhesive under it fails. Drawers and cabinet doors stop lining up.

After a week

Tear Out Likely

Swollen cabinet boxes cannot be brought back. The subfloor may be coming apart in layers, and mold behind cabinets often means they come out together with the countertop.

What We Do

Our Kitchen Water Damage Process

1

Shut Off the Leak

First we close the line to the leaking appliance or fixture, slide out the dishwasher or fridge when needed, and identify the exact failed connection so the repair can happen ahead of drying.

2

Measure the Wet Area

Using moisture meters and thermal imaging, we check cabinet bases, the wall behind them, and the flooring across the room. Water under a kitchen floor often travels farther than expected.

3

Dry in Place

Toe kicks come off and air is directed under and behind the cabinets. Air movers and dehumidifiers run for several days, and we treat damp cavities with an antimicrobial.

4

Remove Only Damage

We carefully remove any flooring, subfloor or cabinet section that cannot be saved. After dry readings are confirmed, the appliances go back in and we help you arrange the remaining repairs.
