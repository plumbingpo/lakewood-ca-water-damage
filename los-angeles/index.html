Simple Ways to Keep a Los Angeles Home Dry

A large share of the jobs we attend in Los Angeles began as a minor fault that could have been caught. Looking over a short list twice a year is enough to find most of those faults.

Start with supply lines. Washer, dishwasher, toilet and refrigerator lines made of plain rubber age badly, and swapping them for braided steel is a cheap fix. Open each sink cabinet and check for water marks, a warped base or a damp odor. At the water heater, watch for corrosion or moisture around the bottom, and flush some sediment out annually. Outside, keep gutters clear and make sure downspouts send water away from the foundation.

Find the main water shutoff now, and show every adult in the home how to close it. Small battery leak alarms near the washing machine, water heater and sinks give you an early warning for very little money. Common Water Damage Issues in Los Angeles

In Los Angeles, the same handful of problems accounts for nearly all the water damage we see. If you can spot these early, you have a good chance of stopping the damage before it spreads.

Pipes: When water freezes inside a line it can crack the metal or plastic, then pour out as soon as things warm up. Depending on the season, the plumbing most at risk runs through outside walls, attics and garages without heat. In older homes, aging joints and tired connectors sometimes fail with no cold snap involved. Your first move is closing the main water valve, since a broken pipe floods room after room fast.

Appliances: Washing machines, dishwashers and refrigerator ice makers all depend on small hoses that wear out. Tanks rust from the inside, so an old water heater may drip for weeks or give out suddenly. These failures do the most harm when the appliance is on an upper floor or above finished living space.

Overflows: A clogged toilet or a sink left running can put water on the floor in minutes. The water often runs under baseboards and through the ceiling of the room below. If the overflow came from the toilet bowl or a drain, treat it as contaminated and keep children and pets away.

Roof: Rain gets in through worn flashing, damaged shingles or gutters that have stopped draining. The stain on your ceiling usually shows up well after the insulation above it is soaked. We dry the framing, insulation and drywall, and a roofer needs to fix the opening so it does not return.

Basement: Once the ground outside is soaked, water finds its way into a basement through wall cracks and the seam where floor meets wall. If the sump pump sticks or the electricity goes out mid-storm, the pit fills and spills across the slab. Carpet, wall studs and drywall wick moisture upward, which is why finished lower levels take the worst of it.

Mold: Damp drywall, wood and carpet can begin to grow mold in as little as 24 to 48 hours. Many mold jobs we handle in Los Angeles began with an old leak that was wiped up but never dried inside the wall. Have it looked at if you notice a stale odor, bubbling paint or dark specks around an earlier leak.
